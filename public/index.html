Clean, cool, dry oil

Most bearing and gearbox failures start with contaminated or wrong lubricant. In the lube room I keep oil clean, dry and correctly identified before it ever reaches a machine.

FiltrationKidney-loop filtering through 10 and 3 micron elements until the oil reaches its ISO 4406 cleanliness target.
Moisture controlDesiccant breathers stop humid air getting in. The beads change colour as they absorb water, so I know when to swap them.
IdentificationColour-coded tags on every drum, container, grease gun and fill point, so the right oil goes in the right machine.

Catch it early: the P-F curve

From the first sign of a problem (P) to functional failure (F), each technique sees trouble at a different point. Oil analysis, ultrasound and vibration see it months ahead. Noise and heat give days. That early window is where I work.

Reliability through better lubrication

I'm a Mechanical Engineer with more than three years in asset reliability, machinery lubrication, predictive maintenance and vibration-based condition monitoring, across Australia's paper manufacturing and aluminium smelting industries.

I'm currently Lubrication Supervisor at JW Engineering, an internal contractor at Visy's Smithfield paper mill (3 & 6). I lead lubrication management and maintenance coordination, supervising lubrication technicians, mechanical trades and subcontractors through routine work and shutdowns.

My technical work brings together hands-on mechanical engineering, vibration analysis, lubrication engineering and condition monitoring. I use it to find equipment problems early, improve maintenance strategies and cut unplanned downtime. I also sit on ICML's Exam Development Committee, helping keep the MLE certification exam accurate and fair.

I like solving hard machinery problems and turning condition-monitoring data into maintenance decisions that hold up on the plant floor.
